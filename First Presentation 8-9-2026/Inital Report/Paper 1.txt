  - Meaning: Linear dimensionality reduction technique projecting data onto orthogonal axes (principal components) maximizing variance.

6. Possible Viva Questions
• Q1 (Basic): What does TF-IDF stand for and how is it calculated?
  - Model Answer: Term Frequency - Inverse Document Frequency. It multiplies Term Frequency TF(t,d) (count of term t in doc d) by Inverse Document Frequency IDF(t) = log(N / DF(t)) where N is total documents and DF is document frequency of term t.
• Q2 (Technical): Explain the fundamental architectural difference between CBOW and Continuous Skip-gram in Word2Vec.
  - Model Answer: CBOW takes multiple surrounding context words as input to predict the single target center word. Continuous Skip-gram takes a single target center word as input to predict its surrounding context words.
• Q3 (Conceptual): Why does Bag-of-Words (BOW) lead to the 'curse of dimensionality'?
  - Model Answer: BOW creates a matrix column for every single unique word in the corpus vocabulary, generating immense sparse matrices containing predominantly zeros, which degrades classifier speed and accuracy.
• Q4 (Comparison): Compare TF-IDF weighting against raw Bag-of-Words frequency counting.
  - Model Answer: Raw BOW treats all frequent words equally, giving high weight to non-discriminative common words. TF-IDF penalizes words that occur across many documents, assigning maximum weight to rare, highly specific terms.
• Q5 (Why): Why is PCA applied during the feature reduction phase?
  - Model Answer: To eliminate multi-collinearity and redundancy among text features, compressing sparse, high-dimensional vector representations into dense orthogonal components while preserving maximum statistical variance.

2. Main Contribution of the Paper
• Conducted a systematic empirical comparative evaluation between 3 distinct feature vectorization models (BOW, TF-IDF, Word2Vec) and 4 supervised ML classifiers (LR, Linear SVM, k-NN, Naïve Bayes) on the MTSamples medical transcription dataset.
• Established that dense neural embeddings (Word2Vec) superiorly preserve contextual and semantic relationships in clinical text compared to sparse term-frequency representations (BOW/TF-IDF).
• Proved that combining Word2Vec embeddings with a tuned k-NN classifier achieves a state-of-the-art accuracy of 92% across 20 medical specialties.

3. Limitations of the Paper
• Exclusion of Ensemble Learning: The authors evaluated single classifiers independently but did not explore Ensemble Methods (e.g., Voting Classifiers, Stacking, Random Forest, AdaBoost, XGBoost) which can combine weak/strong learners to surpass single-model accuracy limits.
• Class Loss via Arbitrary Pruning: Eliminating categories with <50 samples discarded half of the available medical categories (reducing from 40 down to 20), reducing real-world clinical coverage.
• Lack of Real-Time Web Deployment: The study is purely experimental/analytical; no accessible web UI or REST API was created for real-time doctor/user interaction.
• Absence of Modern Transformer Baselines: The paper compared classical models against Word2Vec (2013) but omitted modern contextual transformer embeddings like ClinicalBERT or BioBERT.

10. Top 30 Viva Questions from the Entire Paper with Detailed Answers

Q1: What is the core title and research goal of this paper?
Answer: The paper is titled "Clinical Text Classification with Word Representation Features and Machine Learning Algorithms". Its goal is to systematically evaluate how different text representation models (BOW, TF-IDF, Word2Vec) and ML algorithms (LR, SVM, k-NN, NB) perform in categorizing EMR transcriptions into clinical specialties.

Q2: What dataset was used in this research?
Answer: The Medical Transcriptions dataset from mtsamples.com (publicly available on Kaggle).

Q3: Why are public clinical datasets so scarce in computer science research?
Answer: Due to legal patient privacy mandates such as HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act), which strictly prevent the public release of confidential Electronic Medical Records (EMRs).

Q4: What were the raw statistics of the MTSamples dataset before preprocessing?
Answer: Around 40 medical categories, 140,214 sentences, and 35,822 unique vocabulary words.

Q5: How did the authors handle class imbalance and sparse categories in MTSamples?
Answer: They applied a frequency threshold rule, retaining only categories with more than 50 records. This pruned the dataset from 40 down to 20 categories, leaving 4,324 records.

Q6: Name the largest and smallest classes in the filtered dataset (Table 1).
Answer: The largest class is Surgery with 1,088 samples (~25% of data). The smallest class is Psychiatry/Psychology with 53 samples.

Q7: What are the 5 stages of the clinical text classification pipeline shown in Figure 1?
Answer: 1. Dataset Preparation, 2. Text Pre-processing, 3. Word Representation, 4. Features Reduction, 5. Classification.

Q8: What are the specific text preprocessing steps executed in Section 3.2?
Answer: Data cleaning (stripping punctuation, digits, special characters), Text lowering, Stop-words removal, Lemmatization, and Tokenization.

Q9: What is the difference between Stemming and Lemmatization?
Answer: Stemming chops word endings off using fixed rules (often producing non-words like "operat"), whereas Lemmatization uses grammatical rules and dictionary lookup to convert inflected words to valid root lemmas (e.g., "operating" -> "operate").

Q10: Define Bag-of-Words (BOW) and state its primary weakness.
Answer: BOW represents text by counting absolute word occurrences in a document. Its weakness is that it ignores word order/context and generates high-dimensional, ultra-sparse matrices full of zeros.

Q11: What is TF-IDF and how does it improve upon BOW?
Answer: TF-IDF (Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency) weights a word by multiplying its local frequency (TF) by its global corpus rarity (IDF). It improves upon BOW by down-weighting ubiquitous uninformative words and emphasizing rare, highly specific clinical terms.

Q12: How does Word2Vec create vector representations?
Answer: It trains a shallow 2-layer neural network to map words into a dense vector space based on surrounding context, utilizing either Continuous Bag-of-Words (CBOW) or Continuous Skip-gram architectures.

Q13: What is the difference between CBOW and Continuous Skip-gram in Word2Vec?
Answer: CBOW predicts a target center word using surrounding context words. Skip-gram does the opposite: it uses a center target word to predict surrounding context words.

Q14: What technique was used for feature reduction in Section 3.4?
Answer: Principal Component Analysis (PCA), which projects data onto lower-dimensional orthogonal axes to maximize variance while cutting redundant features.

Q15: What train-test split ratio was implemented?
Answer: A 75% training set (3,243 records) and 25% testing set (1,081 records) split.

Q16: What four machine learning algorithms were evaluated in this paper?
Answer: Logistic Regression (LR), Support Vector Machine (SVM), k-Nearest Neighbors (k-NN), and Naïve Bayes (NB).

Q17: Why was a Linear Kernel chosen for SVM instead of RBF?
Answer: Text feature vectors are high-dimensional and sparse, making classes naturally linearly separable. A linear kernel is faster, requires less tuning, and avoids overfitting compared to non-linear kernels like RBF.

Q18: What is the fundamental assumption of the Naïve Bayes classifier?
Answer: The "naïve" assumption of class-conditional independence: it assumes that every word feature occurs independently of all other word features given the class label.

Q19: What parameter needs tuning in the k-NN classifier?
Answer: The parameter k, which defines how many nearest neighbors participate in majority voting when assigning a class label to a new instance.

Q20: Write the mathematical formulas for Precision, Recall, and F1-Score.
Answer: Precision = TP / (TP + FP), Recall = TP / (TP + FN), F1 Score = 2 * (Precision * Recall) / (Precision + Recall).

Q21: What combination achieved the absolute highest accuracy in the paper?
Answer: Word2Vec feature representation + k-Nearest Neighbors (k-NN) classifier, achieving 92% accuracy (0.90 F1 Score).

Q22: What was the best performance achieved using TF-IDF feature extraction?
Answer: TF-IDF + Logistic Regression, achieving 89% accuracy (0.88 F1 Score, 0.88 Precision, 0.89 Recall).

Q23: What general trend was observed across feature representation methods in Table 4?
Answer: Word2Vec consistently outperformed TF-IDF, and TF-IDF consistently outperformed Bag-of-Words across all four ML classifiers.

Q24: Why did Bag-of-Words perform poorly compared to Word2Vec?
Answer: BOW only retains frequency counts in sparse matrices without semantic context. Word2Vec captures dense contextual and linguistic associations between clinical terms.

Q25: What future directions did the authors recommend in Section 5?
Answer: Validating their pipeline on COVID-19 clinical datasets and evaluating modern Deep Learning models (e.g., CNNs, Transformers).

Q26: How does YOUR MCA project build upon this reference paper?
Answer: My project adopts their 5-stage NLP preprocessing pipeline and TF-IDF feature representation, but enhances the classification phase by building a Voting Ensemble Classifier (combining LR, SVM, Random Forest, and Naïve Bayes) and deploying it as a live Flask web application.

Q27: Why did you choose TF-IDF over Word2Vec for your project implementation?
Answer: Word2Vec requires loading heavy pre-trained neural embedding files (multiple gigabytes), causing high memory load and latency. TF-IDF is lightweight, fast to compute, highly interpretable, and perfectly suited for fast web application inference.

Q28: What is an Ensemble Voting Classifier and why is it superior to single classifiers?
Answer: An Ensemble Voting Classifier combines predictions from multiple individual models (e.g., LR, SVM, RF, NB) using majority rule (hard voting) or averaged probabilities (soft voting). It reduces variance and bias, neutralizing individual model errors to achieve higher overall stability.

Q29: Why is a web application UI important for your project?
Answer: In real-world hospital environments, medical personnel need an intuitive graphical interface where they can paste unorganized transcription notes and immediately receive predicted specialties without running terminal code or scripts.
