2. Key Findings
• Production Reliability Barrier: Deep learning models achieve high accuracy overall, but their deployment reliability in real-world production settings is severely hindered by imbalanced datasets where a few majority classes dominate.
  - What it means: High micro-accuracy masks near-zero accuracy on rare medical conditions.
  - Why important: Proves that standard overall accuracy is a deceptive metric for clinical AI systems.
  - Why authors included it: To motivate the need for specialized class imbalance remedies.
• Cause of Overfitting on Rare Classes: Rare classes combine two compounding vulnerabilities: long raw text documents (thousands of words) and very few training samples (fewer than 50). The model learns spurious token combinations unique to those few documents.
  - What it means: The network memorizes specific long-text noise instead of learning true diagnostic signals.
  - Why important: Identifies the root mechanism of model failure in clinical NLP.
  - Why authors included it: To justify injecting short, idealized keyword sequences as model constraints.
• Proposed Solution Overview: Assembling class-associated keywords (short phrases/concepts) and injecting them into mini-batches during training, creating a combined loss function $L = L_{docs} + \alpha L_{key}$.

1. Purpose of the Page
• Why this page exists: Page 2 presents the detailed rationale against class weighting/sampling, introduces the 2 automated keyword extraction methods (UMLS CUIs vs NPMI), defines **Equation 1: Normalized Pointwise Mutual Information (NPMI)**, and details the 6-step mini-batch training algorithm.

• Importance in overall paper: The core algorithmic and mathematical methodology page detailing keyword generation and training integration.

--------------------------------------------------
2. Key Findings
• Limitations of Class Weighting (CW): CW makes predictions of rare classes more likely, but does NOT improve the model's ability to selectively identify meaningful text segments out of long sequences.
  - What it means: CW forces higher probability outputs without improving feature discrimination.
  - Why important: Explains why CW boosts macro F1 only at the cost of destroying micro F1.
• Two Automated Keyword Generation Approaches:
  1. **Ontology-Based (CUIs):** Extract Concept Unique Identifiers (CUIs) from NCI Thesaurus (NCIt) and ICD-O-3 mapped to Unified Medical Language System (UMLS).
  2. **Corpus-Based (NPMI):** Use Normalized Pointwise Mutual Information (NPMI) to rank top 10 unigrams/bigrams per class directly from raw training text.
  - What it means: Keywords can be gathered automatically either from formal medical databases or purely from statistical text data!
• NPMI Mathematical Formulation (Equation 1):
  $$\text{NPMI} = \frac{-1}{\log(p(x,y))} \log\left(\frac{p(x,y)}{p(x)p(y)}\right)$$
  - Normalizes standard PMI to $[-1, +1]$ scale, where $+1$ means token $x$ and class $y$ always co-occur.
• Six-Step Keyword Mini-Batch Training Update:
  1. Calculate document cross-entropy loss $L_{docs}$.
  2. Sample $N_C$ classes.
  3. Sample $K$ keyword segments per class.
  4. Concatenate $K$ segments into $N_C$ keyword documents.
  5. Calculate keyword loss $L_{key}$.
  6. Backpropagate total combined loss: $L = L_{docs} + \alpha L_{key}$.
• Optimal Hyperparameters: $K = 5$ keyword segments per class and loss weight $\alpha = 1.0$.

  - Purpose: Forces backpropagation gradients to update weights based on both full notes and short keywords.
  - Relation to paper: Core optimization equation.

11. Top 30 Viva Questions
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q1: What is the main title and research objective of this IEEE paper?
Answer: "A Keyword-Enhanced Approach to Handle Class Imbalance in Clinical Text Classification". Goal: Improve classification accuracy on rare clinical classes by incorporating short keyword sequences into mini-batch training.

Q2: Why do deep learning models overfit on rare classes in clinical text?
Answer: Because rare classes combine long raw text sequences (thousands of words) with very few training samples (fewer than 50), causing the model to memorize document-specific noise.

Q3: Why is Class Weighting (CW) inferior to Keyword Training?
Answer: Class weighting increases loss penalties but fails to teach the model which short text segments define a class, boosting Macro F1 only by over-predicting rare labels and dropping Micro F1.

Q4: What are the two keyword generation approaches evaluated?
Answer: 1. Ontology-driven extraction using UMLS CUIs, and 2. Corpus-driven statistical ranking using Normalized Pointwise Mutual Information (NPMI).

Q5: Write down the NPMI formula from Equation 1.
Answer: $\text{NPMI} = \frac{-1}{\log(p(x,y))} \log\left(\frac{p(x,y)}{p(x)p(y)}\right)$.

Q6: What scale does NPMI operate on and what does $+1$ signify?
Answer: Scale is $[-1, +1]$. A score of $+1$ means token $x$ and class $y$ always co-occur.

Q7: Write down the combined mini-batch loss function.
Answer: $L = L_{docs} + \alpha L_{key}$, where $L_{docs}$ is document loss, $L_{key}$ is keyword loss, and $\alpha=1.0$.

Q8: What are the optimal values for hyperparameters $K$ and $\alpha$?
Answer: $K=5$ keyword segments per class and loss weight $\alpha=1.0$.

Q9: What datasets were evaluated in the paper?
Answer: 1. Development dataset (177,185 pathology reports), and 2. Production dataset (4,404,942 pathology reports) from NCI SEER registries.

Q10: What 3 clinical tasks were evaluated?
Answer: Cancer site (70 classes), subsite (315-328 classes), and histology (534-645 classes).

Q11: Describe the 1D CNN architecture used in Section II-D.
Answer: 300d Word2Vec embeddings -> 3 parallel 1D conv layers (300 filters each, window sizes 3, 4, 5) -> Maxpool-over-time -> 900d document vector -> Softmax.

Q12: What is the difference between Micro F1 and Macro F1?
Answer: Micro F1 aggregates all document instances globally (dominated by majority classes). Macro F1 averages per-class F1 scores equally, making it essential for evaluating rare classes.
