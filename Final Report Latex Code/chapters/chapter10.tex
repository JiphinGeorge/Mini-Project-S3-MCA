\subsection{Minimum Hardware Requirements}

The architectural design prioritized computational accessibility, ensuring that the system can be trained and operated in clinical environments without requiring expensive, power-hungry Graphics Processing Units (GPUs). Table \ref{tab:hardware_requirements} outlines the minimum and recommended hardware specifications.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{3.8cm}|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{4.0cm}|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X|}
\hline
\textbf{Hardware Component} & \textbf{Minimum Specification} & \textbf{Recommended Production Specification} \\
\hline
Processor (CPU) & Dual-Core 64-bit Processor \newline (Intel Core i3 or AMD Ryzen 3, \newline Clock speed $\ge 2.0$ GHz) & Quad-Core / Octa-Core Processor \newline (Intel Core i5/i7 or AMD Ryzen 5/7, \newline Clock speed $\ge 3.0$ GHz) \\
\hline
System Memory (RAM) & 4 GB DDR4 RAM \newline (Active process takes $< 150$ MB) & 8 GB to 16 GB DDR4/DDR5 RAM \newline (Supports high-concurrency client traffic) \\
\hline
Storage (Disk Space) & 2 GB free storage space \newline (Dataset + Models require 25 MB) & 20 GB free Solid-State Drive (SSD) \newline (For application logging and hospital backups) \\
\hline
Graphics Processing Unit & \textbf{None Required} \newline (Zero GPU dependency; executes entirely on standard CPU) & Optional dedicated GPU \newline (Only if expanding to future multimodal ViT or Vision models) \\
\hline
Network Interface & Standard 10/100 Mbps Ethernet or 802.11n Wi-Fi adapter & Gigabit Ethernet (1000 Mbps) \newline (For enterprise hospital intranet deployment) \\
\hline
Display Resolution & $1280 \times 720$ (HD) display resolution & $1920 \times 1080$ (Full HD) or higher \newline (Optimized for side-by-side clinical triage) \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\caption{Minimum and Recommended Hardware Specifications}
\label{tab:hardware_requirements}
\end{table}

As highlighted in Table \ref{tab:hardware_requirements}, the entire platform functions efficiently on entry-level commercial workstations with minimal resource contention.
