\begin{center}
    \textbf{\Large ABSTRACT}
\end{center}
\vspace{0.4cm}

The rapid digitisation of modern healthcare has led to an exponential influx of unstructured clinical narratives within Electronic Health Record (EHR) systems, including operative reports, consultation summaries, and discharge notes. Manually triaging these clinical texts into medical specialties is labor-intensive, error-prone, and bottlenecked by inter-annotator variability. This project develops and deploys \textbf{MedSpecialty AI}, an automated clinical decision-support platform for medical specialty classification using sublinear Term Frequency--Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF) feature engineering paired with an Ensemble Machine Learning architecture.

The system utilizes the MTSamples clinical transcription corpus (4,999 raw records across 40 categories). Systematic exploratory data analysis revealed procedural ambiguity in administrative categories (e.g., General Surgery, Consult notes). A curated benchmark of 1,663 records across eight mutually exclusive hospital departments was established: Cardiovascular / Pulmonary, Orthopedic, Gastroenterology, Neurology, Urology, Obstetrics / Gynecology, ENT -- Otolaryngology, and Ophthalmology. The NLP pipeline performs text cleaning, stopword removal, morphological lemmatization, and sublinear TF-IDF vectorization across an 8,000-dimensional unigram/bigram vocabulary ($L_2$ normalized). Four estimators were evaluated under stratified holdout validation (80\% train / 20\% test): Linear SVM (with Platt calibration, 88.59\% accuracy), Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Multinomial Naive Bayes. A Soft Voting Ensemble combining all four models [2:2:1:1] achieved 87.99\% accuracy and a Macro F1-score of 0.8898 with calibrated posterior probabilities.

To guarantee patient safety in open-world hospital workflows, the platform incorporates an \textbf{Open-Set Confidence Rejection Gate} ($\tau = 0.48$). Dictations with peak ensemble confidence below 48\% are routed to a 9th operational class, \textit{``Other / Requires Review''}, intercepting unsupported clinical specialties (e.g., Dermatology, Psychiatry) and non-medical text without false specialty force-fitting. The platform is operationalized as a responsive web service featuring in-memory PyMuPDF PDF extraction, 7-phase operational dataflow tracking, and sub-10 ms CPU inference latency.
