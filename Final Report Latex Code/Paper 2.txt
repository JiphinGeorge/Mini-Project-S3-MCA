• Section 3.1 Notions & Mathematical Notation:
  - Original Dataset: $D_{orig} = \{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$ where text $x_i = \{w_1, w_2, \dots, w_m\}$.
  - Softmax Confidence: $F_y(x)$ represents probability output for class $y$.
  - Adversarial Example: $x_{adv} = \{w_1', w_2', \dots, w_q'\}$ generated via imperceptible perturbation $\Delta x$.
  - Augmented Dataset: $D_{ada} = D_{orig} \cup D_{adv}$.
• Four-Step Adversarial Augmentation Protocol:
  1. Train base model $F_{base}$ on $D_{orig}$.
  2. Generate semiadversarial examples $x_{adv}'$ for each text in $D_{orig}$.
  3. Construct adversarial dataset $D_{adv} = \{(x_{adv}', y_i)\}$.
  4. Train final model $F$ on $D_{ada} = D_{orig} \cup D_{adv}$.
• Multiple-Step Adversarial Attack (MSAA): Unlike single-step attacks that perturb one word at a time, MSAA perturbs multiple words per attack step to generate rich candidate combinations.

## 1. Purpose of the Page

• Why this page exists: Page 4 details the exact 3-step mechanism of SemiADA, introduces **Figure 1: Visual illustrative example**, and presents the mathematical equations for Wording Importance Ranking (Equation 1) and Candidate Set Selection (Equation 2).
• What the authors are trying to explain: How words are ranked by importance using gradient projection, how candidate word combinations are sampled efficiently from WordNet/GloVe, and how semiadversarial examples are retained while full adversarial examples are discarded.
• Importance in overall paper: The key technical page detailing the SemiADA algorithm and decision-space geometry.

--------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Key Points

• Figure 1 Breakdown (Decision Space Geometry):
  - (a) Standard Data Augmentation: Generates samples clustered tightly around original data points (limited coverage).
  - (b) Adversarial Attack: Pushes samples all the way across the decision boundary.
  - (c) Semi-Adversarial Data Augmentation (SemiADA): Generates samples in the yellow-shaded intermediate space right up to the boundary, covering a much larger decision space while preserving true labels.
• Step 1: Wording Importance Ranking (Equation 1):
  $$I(w_i, x) = (V_{[MASK]} - V_{w_i}) \nabla_{w_i} J(\theta, x, f(x))$$
  - Meaning: Measures how much word $w_i$ affects model loss $J$ by projecting the vector difference between $[MASK]$ embedding and $w_i$ embedding onto the gradient direction.
  - Why Important: Requires querying the model only **once** to rank all words, boosting computational efficiency compared to deleting words one by one.
  - Stop words (NLTK/SpaCy) are filtered out after ranking.
• Step 2: Candidate Word Combinations (Equation 2):
  - Vocabulary dictionary constructed from $D_{orig}$ contains 27,816 words.
  - Select top $k$ synonyms from WordNet using GloVe cosine similarity.
  - Select top $t$ important words per attack step ($j$-th attack step perturbs words $i$ to $i+t$).
  - Sample $r = k \times t$ candidate combinations to avoid exponential complexity ($k^t$).
• Step 3: Construct Semiadversarial Examples:
  - Replace words in $x$ with candidates.
  - **Filtering Rule:** If prediction probability on original label $y$ drops ($F_y(x') < F_y(x_{adv})$) BUT prediction label does not change ($F(x') = y$), add $x'$ to augmented set! Discard if label flips ($F(x') \neq y$).

  - Meaning: Unsupervised learning algorithm developed by Stanford for obtaining vector representations for words.
  - Purpose: Pretrained word embeddings for semantic similarity scoring.
  - Relation to paper: Provides word vectors for computing WordNet synonym similarity.

## 2. Key Points

• Algorithm 1 (SemiADA Pseudocode):
  - Inputs: Medical note $x$, true label $y$, target model $F$, attack step size $t$, synonym count $k$, dataset $D_{orig}$.
  - Sorts words by importance via Eq (1), filters stop words, loops through word chunks of size $t$.
  - Extracts top $k$ synonyms from WordNet, randomly samples candidates, replaces words, checks probability condition, and appends valid semiadversarial samples to `AESet`.
• Time Complexity Analysis (Section 3.2.1):
  - Formula: $T(n) = O(\frac{n}{t}(t + kt + kt)) = (2k+1)n$.
  - Because $k$ is a constant, execution time grows **linearly** $O(n)$ with input text length $n$.
  - Mainstream black-box adversarial attacks take $O(n^2)$ or higher. SemiADA is at least one order of magnitude faster!
• Probabilistic Information (PI) Layer Motivation (Section 3.3):
  - Data augmentation improves minority class accuracy, but accuracy under supermultiple categories (18 classes) remains unsatisfactory.
  - High-frequency medical nouns carry class-specific signals (e.g., "stomach" appears in 'gastroenterology', never in 'podiatry').
• Word Category Importance (WCI) Formula (Equation 4):
  $$\text{WCI}(w_i, y) = \text{IDF}' \times \frac{(k+1) \times \text{TF}'}{k \left(1 - b + b \frac{|D_y|}{\text{avg } |D_{Y_j}|}\right) + \text{TF}'}$$
  - Inspired by the **BM25 algorithm** from information retrieval.
  - Balances term frequency $\text{TF}'$ in class $y$, class sample size $|D_y|$, and inverse document frequency $\text{IDF}'$.

## 2. Key Points

• Equations 5 to 8 (PI Layer Mathematics):
  - Equation 5 ($\text{IDF}'$): $\text{IDF}'(w_i, y) = \log\left(\frac{|D|}{|D_{w_i}| - |D_{y,w_i}| + 1}\right) + (1-a)\frac{|D_{y,w_i}|}{|D_y|}$ with damping factor $a$.
  - Equation 6 ($\text{TF}'$): $\text{TF}' = \frac{C_{y,w_i}}{C_y}$ (word occurrences in class $y$ divided by total words in class $y$).
  - Equation 7 (Category Propensity Score): $\text{Score}(x, y_i) = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{w_i \in x} \text{WCI}(w_i, y_i)^2}{n}}$.
  - Equation 8 (Probabilistic Distribution Recalculation Post-Softmax):
    $$\text{Softmax-PI}(z_i) = \frac{e^{z_i \cdot \text{Score}(x, y_i) - M}}{\sum_{j=1}^c e^{z_j \cdot \text{Score}(x, y_j) - M}}$$
    where $M = \max(z_j \cdot \text{Score}(x, y_j))$ prevents numerical overflow.
• Figure 2 Architecture:

FINAL ANALYSIS
================================================================================

1. Paper Summary
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This paper addresses the challenge of classifying unstructured Electronic Health Record (EHR) notes into 18 medical specialty categories under severe data insufficiency and class imbalance. The authors propose a two-part framework:
1. **SemiADA (Semi-Adversarial Data Augmentation):** Repurposes adversarial attacks to generate synthetic training text. By capturing intermediate perturbed samples right near decision boundaries before they flip true class labels, SemiADA expands decision space coverage.
2. **Probabilistic Information (PI) Layer:** Placed after Softmax, this layer recalculates output probability distributions by scaling logits with a BM25-derived Word Category Importance (WCI) metric focused on medical nouns.
Evaluated on an 18-class Kaggle dataset, BioBERT trained with SemiADA + PI achieved **87.7% Accuracy and 88.2% Micro-F1**, outperforming baseline models by 14.9% while significantly improving adversarial robustness.


2. Research Problem
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• **Target Problem:** High-accuracy medical specialty text classification on Electronic Health Records (EHRs) containing severely imbalanced and insufficient data across 18 specialty categories.
• **Why Important:** Automated specialty tagging enables fast medical record retrieval, aids general practitioners in triage, and supports clinical referral analysis. However, medical notes are difficult to acquire due to HIPAA privacy, and rare specialties (e.g., Podiatry, Office Notes) suffer from severe data scarcity.


3. Existing Problems
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• **Text Data Augmentation Deficiencies:** Simple character/word deletion or random swapping fails on discrete text, breaking syntax and altering medical meanings.
• **Small Category Scope in Literature:** Prior medical text classification studies evaluated at most 9 specialty categories, failing to address realistic multi-class clinical scenarios.
• **Label Flipping in Adversarial Training:** Standard adversarial data augmentation (ADA using TextFooler) generates full adversarial samples that cross decision boundaries, flipping true labels and degrading model accuracy down to 72.1%.


4. Proposed Solution
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The proposed solution consists of 6 sequential steps:
1. **Model Initialization:** Train a base victim classifier (BioBERT) on clean training data $D_{orig}$.
2. **Gradient-Based Wording Importance Ranking:** Compute loss gradient projections to rank word importance in a single query pass (Equation 1).
3. **Synonym Candidate Sampling:** Extract top $k$ WordNet synonyms using GloVe cosine similarity and sample $r = k \times t$ combinations (Equation 2).
4. **Semi-Adversarial Sample Filtering:** Substitute words; retain perturbed sample $x'$ ONLY IF prediction confidence drops BUT predicted class label remains unchanged ($F(x') = y$).
5. **Probabilistic Information (PI) Layer Calculation:** Compute BM25-based Word Category Importance (WCI) for medical nouns (Equation 4) and multiply with Softmax logits (Equation 8).
6. **Final Model Retraining:** Retrain BioBERT on merged dataset $D_{ada} = D_{orig} \cup D_{adv}$ with the active PI layer.


5. Complete Workflow
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
```
[Raw EHR Medical Notes (3,140 records across 18 categories)]
                           │
                           ▼
[Text Preprocessing: Noise Removal ──► NLTK/SpaCy Stop-word Filtering]
                           │
                           ▼
[BioBERT Base Training ──► Loss Function J Calculation]
                           │
                           ▼
[Step 1: Wording Importance Ranking via Gradient Projection Eq(1)]
                           │
                           ▼
[Step 2: WordNet/GloVe Synonym Candidate Sampling Eq(2)]
                           │
                           ▼
[Step 3: SemiADA Filtering ──► Keep x' if F_y(x') < F_y(x) AND F(x') == y]
                           │
                           ▼
[Augmented Dataset Construction: D_ada = D_orig ∪ D_adv]
                           │
                           ▼
[BioBERT Fine-Tuning + FC Layer ──► Softmax Output]
                           │
                           ▼
[Post-Softmax PI Layer: BM25-derived WCI Noun Weighting Eq(4-8)]
                           │
                           ▼
[Final Output Prediction: 87.7% Accuracy / 88.2% Micro-F1]
```


6. Novel Contributions
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. **SemiADA Framework:** First data augmentation method to systematically capture *semiadversarial* text samples near decision boundaries without crossing them, preserving true label semantics.
2. **Linear-Time MSAA Attack:** Multiple-step adversarial attack method with $O((2k+1)n)$ linear time complexity, running 4x faster than standard $O(n^2)$ attacks (12 mins vs 67 mins).
3. **Post-Softmax PI Layer:** Novel post-processing layer using BM25-derived noun category importance (WCI) to recalculate Softmax probability distributions, specifically boosting minority class recall.
4. **Adversarial Robustness Enhancement:** Proved that semiadversarial training slashes TextFooler attack success rates from 72.4% down to 26.2%.


7. Dataset Analysis
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• **Source:** Kaggle Medical Specialty Classification competition dataset (`kaggle.com/competitions/medical-specialty-classification`).
• **Raw Stats:** Unstructured clinical notes covering consultation notes, surgical procedures, and progress notes.
• **Filtered Stats:** Categories with $<30$ notes removed -> **3,140 notes across 18 medical categories**.
• **Class Distribution (Figure 3):** Severely imbalanced. Surgery dominates with 750+ samples; Consult - History & Phy has ~310 samples; Podiatry and Office Notes have under 35 samples.
• **Text Length Distribution (Figure 4):** Most notes range between **180 and 230 words** (max 1,200 words).
• **Why Imbalance Exists:** Reflects real-world hospital operational reality where surgical and general consultation notes occur far more frequently than specialized subspecialty notes.


8. Complete Model Explanation
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• **BioBERT:** Pre-trained biomedical language model based on BERT, initialized with 12 transformer layers, 12 attention heads, and 768 hidden dimensions. It converts input medical text into rich 768-dimensional contextual vectors.
  ```
  [Input Text] ──► [BioBERT (12 Layers, 768-dim)] ──► [[CLS] Hidden Vector]
  ```
• **SemiADA:** Data augmentation module that generates synthetic text by perturbing words along adversarial loss gradients while restricting samples to the true label side of the decision boundary.
• **Probabilistic Information (PI) Layer:** A post-Softmax module that calculates Word Category Importance (WCI) using a BM25 variation and multiplies raw class logits by category propensity scores $\text{Score}(x, y_i)$.
  ```
  [Softmax Logits z_i] ──⊗── [Score(x, y_i) from WCI] ──► [Recalculated Softmax-PI]
  ```
• **Confidence Recalculation:** Adjusts output probabilities using Equation 8 to prevent dominant classes from suppressing valid minority class predictions.
• **Adversarial Attack (MSAA):** Multiple-Step Adversarial Attack that modifies $t$ words simultaneously, querying loss gradients once per step.
• **WordNet & GloVe:** WordNet provides synonym sets ($Syn_w$); GloVe 300-dimensional embeddings compute cosine similarity to select the top $k$ closest synonyms.
• **Transformer Encoder & [CLS] Token:** The $[CLS]$ token state output by the 12th transformer layer serves as the aggregate sentence embedding used for t-SNE visualization.


9. Experimental Analysis
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• **Model Comparison (Table 2):** Evaluated CNN, LSTM, BERT, and BioBERT. Proposed SemiADA + PI boosted accuracy across ALL models (+11% to +14.7%), proving model-agnostic effectiveness. BioBERT reached top score: **87.7% Accuracy, 88.2% F1**.
• **Augmentation Benchmark Comparison (Table 3):** Compared Plain (73.0%), SRA (80.3%), ERA (80.9%), LMA (84.9%), ADA (72.1%), and SemiADA + PI (87.7%). Proved SemiADA outperforms all standard text augmentation strategies.
• **Attack Speed Comparison (Table 1):** MSAA took 12 mins and 240 queries vs BERT-Attack's 67 mins and 1,089 queries.
• **Robustness Evaluation (Table 5):** Under TextFooler attack, SemiADA + PI dropped Attack Success Rate (ASR) from 72.4% down to 26.2%, proving enhanced model security.


10. Ablation Study
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• **Purpose:** To isolate the individual contributions of SemiADA and the PI layer.
• **Setup (Table 4 on BioBERT):**
  - *Plain Model:* 73.0% Acc, 71.5% Micro-R, 74.7% Micro-P, 73.1% Micro-F1.
  - *PI Layer Only:* 73.4% Acc, **78.1% Micro-R** (+6.6% recall jump in minority classes!).
  - *SemiADA Only:* 85.6% Acc, 83.2% Micro-R, 86.9% Micro-P, 86.9% Micro-F1 (+12.6% raw boost).
  - *SemiADA + PI (Combined):* **87.7% Acc, 88.6% Micro-R, 87.9% Micro-P, 88.2% Micro-F1**.
• **Conclusion:** SemiADA provides the primary accuracy boost, while the PI layer specifically fixes minority class recall.


11. Robustness Analysis
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• **Why Important:** Clinical AI systems deployed in hospitals must resist noisy, altered, or adversarial text inputs.
• **Testing Protocol:** Tested against 4 attack libraries (TextFooler, DeepWordBug, TextBugger, BERT-Attack) using 200 test notes with max perturbation rate 0.1 and similarity threshold 0.84.
• **Results (Table 5):**
  - TextFooler ASR dropped from 72.4% to 26.2%.
  - TextBugger ASR dropped from 43.0% to 24.9%.
  - DeepWordBug ASR dropped from 48.6% to 32.0%.
• **Conclusion:** Training on boundary-adjacent semiadversarial samples makes the classifier robust against text perturbations.


12. Strengths
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• Repurposes adversarial attacks constructively for text data augmentation.
• Linear time complexity $O(n)$, running 4x faster than standard attacks.
• Solves minority class recall using a domain-specific BM25 noun weighting formula (PI layer).
• Model-agnostic; boosts CNN, LSTM, BERT, and BioBERT architectures.
• Evaluated on a broad 18-class dataset with comprehensive robustness testing.


13. Limitations
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• **High Computational Footprint:** BioBERT (12 layers, 768-dim) requires 4x RTX 2080 GPUs and significant VRAM, making physical edge deployment difficult.
• **Fixed Hyperparameter Reliance:** Requires manual tuning of synonym set size ($k=20$) and attack step size ($t=3$).
• **Class Pruning:** Pruned categories with $<30$ samples, discarding rare clinical edge cases.
• **Lack of Real-Time Interface:** Purely experimental work with no web UI or REST API for clinicians.


14. Future Work
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• Exploring lightweight models suitable for resource-constrained physical medical devices.
• Integrating fuzzy classifiers to handle vague, uncertain clinical phrasing.
• Automating hyperparameter selection ($k$ and $t$) dynamically per dataset.

Q1: What is the main title and objective of Paper 2?
Answer: "Medical Specialty Classification Based on Semiadversarial Data Augmentation". Goal: Solve clinical text data imbalance across 18 medical specialties using SemiADA data augmentation and a post-Softmax PI layer.

Q2: What primary dataset was evaluated in this paper?
Answer: The Kaggle Medical Specialty Classification competition dataset (`kaggle.com/competitions/medical-specialty-classification`).

Q3: What were the cleaned dataset statistics after filtering?
Answer: 3,140 clinical notes across 18 medical categories (classes with <30 notes were pruned).

Q4: Define a 'semiadversarial example' as introduced in this paper.
Answer: An intermediate perturbed text sample generated during an adversarial attack that approaches the model's decision boundary to expand decision space coverage without crossing the boundary or altering the true class label.

Q5: What is the main drawback of standard text data augmentation (e.g., random word deletion)?
Answer: Text is discrete; random modifications easily break sentence syntax, introduce invalid medical jargon, or alter clinical meanings.

Q6: How does SemiADA differ geometrically from standard data augmentation in Figure 1?
Answer: Standard augmentation only generates synthetic points tightly clustered around original data points. SemiADA fills the entire decision space right up to the decision boundary.

Q7: What equation calculates Wording Importance Ranking in Step 1 of SemiADA?
Answer: Equation 1: $I(w_i, x) = (V_{[MASK]} - V_{w_i}) \nabla_{w_i} J(\theta, x, f(x))$, which computes loss gradient projections in a single model query.

Q8: What is the time complexity of the proposed MSAA attack method?
Answer: $T(n) = O((2k+1)n)$, which is linear $O(n)$ with input text length $n$, running at least one order of magnitude faster than standard $O(n^2)$ attacks.

Q9: What filtering condition determines if a perturbed sample $x'$ is added to the augmented set?
Answer: If prediction probability for true label $y$ drops ($F_y(x') < F_y(x)$) AND the predicted class label remains unchanged ($F(x') = y$). If the label flips, it is discarded.

Q10: Why are full adversarial examples (where label flips) excluded from the augmented dataset?
Answer: Full adversarial examples introduce wrong labels into training data, distorting decision boundaries and degrading accuracy down to 72.1%.

Q11: What is the purpose of the Probabilistic Information (PI) layer?
Answer: Placed after Softmax, it recalculates output probability distributions by scaling logits with medical noun category scores, boosting recall for data-insufficient minority classes.

Q12: What classical retrieval algorithm inspired the Word Category Importance (WCI) formula in Equation 4?
Answer: The Okapi BM25 information retrieval algorithm.

Q13: What baseline victim model achieved the top performance in Table 2?
Answer: Fine-tuned BioBERT, achieving **87.7% Accuracy and 88.2% Micro-F1** when trained with SemiADA + PI.

Q14: What were the hyperparameters used for BioBERT fine-tuning?
Answer: 12 transformer layers, 768 hidden size, dropout 0.1, max seq length 512, batch size 16, learning rate 1e-5, 10 epochs.

Q15: What were the 4 baseline neural architectures evaluated in Table 2?
Answer: CNN, LSTM, BERT, and BioBERT.

Q16: What performance gain did SemiADA + PI achieve over plain training across models in Table 2?
Answer: An average improvement of 11% to 14.7% in accuracy.

Q17: What average word length peak was observed for medical notes in Figure 4?
Answer: 180 to 230 words per note.

Q18: What were the 5 baseline data augmentation methods evaluated in Table 3?
Answer: Plain Training, Synonym Replacement (SRA), Embedding Replacement (ERA), Language Model Augmentation (LMA), and Adversarial Data Augmentation (ADA).

Q19: Which augmentation baseline performed second best in Table 3?
Answer: Language Model Augmentation (LMA using Conditional BERT), achieving 84.9% accuracy.

Q20: What happened when Adversarial Data Augmentation (ADA using TextFooler) was applied in Table 3?
Answer: Accuracy degraded to 72.1% (worse than plain training at 73.0%) due to label flipping.

Q21: What were the optimal hyperparameter values found in Sections 5.2–5.4?
Answer: Synonym set size $k=20$, attack step size $t=3$ words per step, and augmented data volume = 5,000 samples per category.

Q22: What happens if you increase $k$ beyond 20 in Figure 5?
Answer: Accuracy plateaus or slightly decreases because distant, semantically noisy synonyms enter candidate sets.

Q23: What happens if you add more than 5,000 augmented samples per category in Figure 7?
Answer: Test accuracy plateaus at 87.7% while training accuracy continues rising, leading to model overfitting.

Q24: What metrics were reported in Table 5 to evaluate adversarial robustness?
Answer: AUA % (Accuracy Under Attack) and ASR % (Attack Success Rate).

Q25: How much did SemiADA + PI reduce the TextFooler Attack Success Rate (ASR) in Table 5?
Answer: Reduced TextFooler ASR from 72.4% (Plain) down to 26.2%.

Q26: What visualization technique was used in Figures 8 and 9 to compare sentence embeddings?
Answer: t-SNE (t-Distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding) projected onto 2D plots.

Q27: What did the t-SNE plots in Figure 8 prove regarding SemiADA versus SRA?
Answer: SRA synthetic points clustered in tiny spots, whereas SemiADA points covered a significantly wider decision space area without overlapping other classes.

Q28: What critical limitation of deep pretrained transformers is acknowledged in Section 5.7?
Answer: Huge model architectures consume excessive GPU memory and storage, making them inconvenient for physical medical deployment.

Q29: What prediction do the authors make regarding future medical AI models in Section 5.7?
Answer: They predict that **lightweight models will be more popular in the medical industry** due to low storage footprints and high interpretability.

Q30: How does Section 5.7 validate YOUR MCA project architecture?
Answer: Our project uses a lightweight TF-IDF Ensemble framework optimized for CPU web deployment, directly matching their call for lightweight clinical AI solutions.

Q31: What is the difference between Micro-F1 and Macro-F1?
Answer: Micro-F1 aggregates total TPs, FPs, and FNs across all classes globally before computing F1. Macro-F1 calculates F1 independently per class and averages them equally.

Q32: What is a [CLS] token in BERT/BioBERT?
Answer: The special classification token prepended to input sequences whose final hidden state serves as the aggregate sentence embedding.

Q33: What hardware was used to execute experiments in Section 4.1.4?
Answer: 4 NVIDIA GeForce RTX 2080 GPUs.

Q34: What is GloVe and how was it used in Step 2 of SemiADA?
Answer: Global Vectors for Word Representation; its 300-dim pretrained embeddings computed cosine similarity to select top $k$ WordNet synonyms.

Q35: Why is model interpretability crucial in healthcare AI?
Answer: Healthcare has zero fault tolerance; clinicians must understand why a model predicted a specific specialty to trust its output and avoid medical errors.

Q36: What is Stratified K-Fold Cross Validation?
Answer: Cross-validation where each fold maintains the exact class proportion ratios of the original dataset.

Q37: Define Okapi BM25 and its role in Equation 4.
Answer: A non-linear information retrieval ranking function used to calculate Word Category Importance (WCI) by weighting word frequency against category length.
